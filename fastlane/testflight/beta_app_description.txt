Echo turns your DRM-free audiobooks into a study tool: chapter looping, smart rewind, voice memo and photo bookmarks, a synced ePub/PDF reader with on-device auto-alignment, on-device AI narration for text-only books (28 voices), chaptered .m4b export, an Anki-style flashcard system (FSRS-4.5 spaced repetition), and an Apple Watch remote you design yourself — up to 25 buttons across 5 pages, plus hands-free flashcard review and a Pomodoro timer on your wrist.

WHAT BETA MEANS

You're testing real, in-development software. Most things work well day-to-day (the developer uses Echo on a mail route every workday), but you will find rough edges — that's the point. Core import, playback and reading do not require an Echo account. Keep source files backed up as with any beta.

WHAT ECHO NEEDS FROM YOU

• Use it like you actually listen — commute, chores, workouts, bedtime. Real-life interruption is exactly what Echo is built for, so don't baby it.
• When something feels wrong, send feedback right then: take a screenshot, tap Share, and choose "Send Beta Feedback" — it arrives with your device and build info attached.
• For alignment issues, include the book title, narrator, and where the ePub/PDF came from (edition matters — narrators ad-lib and editions drift).
• Trackable bugs and feature ideas: github.com/dfakkeldy/Echo/issues

GOOD FIRST TESTS

1. Load a folder of MP3s or an M4B from Files/iCloud and listen through a few interruptions — does Smart Rewind put you back in context every time?
2. Add the matching ePub and run Auto-Align Chapters; then read along and tap a paragraph to jump the audio.
3. Make a bookmark with a voice memo and one with a photo — does your photo take over the player artwork when you pass it again?
4. Create a flashcard from a bookmark and do a Daily Review on your phone, then on your watch.
5. Design your watch button layout from the phone and drive a whole listening session from your wrist.

PRIVACY IN THE BETA

You can connect Echo to your own Audiobookshelf server to browse and play your library. External AI features send text to the provider you configure. When network access is permitted, importing a book may look up matching public alignment markers, with local fallback for an ordinary lookup failure. This release does not expose public alignment uploads. These connections are optional; they are not needed for local playback and reading.

TestFlight (Apple's beta system) does share crash reports and the feedback you choose to send with the developer; that's TestFlight's standard behavior, not Echo's. Full policy: https://kinnokilabs.com/echo-privacy/
